# Street trees and summer heat in Harbourton

Research packet prepared by the Harbourton City Council Environment Directorate, May 2026.
All data below comes from the directorate's own 2025 monitoring programme.

## Question

Does street-tree canopy measurably reduce daytime heat on residential streets, and what would
it cost to close the canopy gap in the wards that are hottest in summer?

## Method

- 212 temperature loggers mounted at 2.5 m on lamp posts across all 18 wards, recording every
  10 minutes from 1 June to 31 August 2025.
- Canopy cover within a 50 m radius of each logger measured from the 2025 aerial survey.
- Streets grouped into three bands: under 10% canopy, 10-30%, and over 30%.
- Heat-related ambulance call-outs taken from the regional service's anonymised daily counts
  for the city, matched to the daily maximum temperature at the airport weather station.
- Costs from the parks team's 2025/26 planting and establishment contract rates.

## Headline figures for decision-makers

1. **2.4°C cooler.** At 3pm on hot days, streets with over 30% canopy were on average 2.4°C
   cooler than streets with under 10% canopy.
2. **17% more call-outs.** Heat-related ambulance call-outs were 17% higher on days reaching
   30°C or more than on days below 25°C.
3. **£3.1m over five years.** Planting and establishing 4,800 street trees in the six
   lowest-canopy wards would cost £3.1m over five years.

## Supporting detail

- The cooling effect was smaller at night: 0.6°C between the same two bands at 11pm.
- The 10-30% band sat in between, 1.1°C cooler than the under-10% band at 3pm.
- There were 14 days of 30°C or more at the airport station in summer 2025, against a
  2015-2024 average of 6.
- The six lowest-canopy wards (Dockside, Millbrook, Quarry Hill, Southgate, Tanner's Row and
  Westfield) hold 38% of the city's residents aged over 75.
- After the five-year establishment period, maintenance for those 4,800 trees in the six
  lowest-canopy wards would add about £140,000 a year. Annual maintenance for the city-wide
  option has not been estimated.
- Trees take roughly eight to twelve years to reach the canopy size that produced the
  2.4°C difference, so the full benefit is not immediate.

## Limitations

- One summer of data. 2025 was unusually hot, which may overstate the typical benefit.
- Loggers on lamp posts read air temperature at street level, not indoor temperature,
  which is what matters most for older residents.
- Call-outs are city-wide daily counts, so the study cannot tie them to specific streets or
  to canopy cover directly.
- Planting costs assume current contract rates and do not include pavement works on the
  narrowest streets, which the parks team has not yet costed.

## Options the directorate has considered

- **A. Targeted planting** in the six lowest-canopy wards (the £3.1m programme above).
- **B. City-wide planting** at the same density: estimated £9.4m over five years.
- **C. No new planting**, relying on the existing cooling-centre programme during heatwaves.

The directorate recommends option A, with a review of the logger data after three summers.
